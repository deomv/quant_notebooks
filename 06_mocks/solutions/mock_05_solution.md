# Mock 05 — Daily aggregation & timezones: solution

Notebook: `mock_05_daily_aggregation_timezones.ipynb`. Fixed version: `mock_05_fixed.ipynb`.
11 planted problems (plus one crash the author "patched"). Kinds: **bug** = wrong output,
**choice** = runs but methodologically wrong, **suspicious** = a result you should refuse to accept.

| # | Where | Kind | Why it matters | How to detect | Fix | Likely follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Timestamps in the feed are local UK time." — `pd.to_datetime(df["time"])` | choice / bug | The feed is UTC. Every "local" statement downstream (peak hour, 17–20 window, day boundaries) is off by one hour for 7 months of the year. | The clean reference file carries `+00:00`. A local feed would have no 01:00 on the last Sunday of March and two 01:00 rows on the last Sunday of October; here `df.loc["2023-03-26 01:00"]` exists and `df.loc["2022-10-30 01:00"]` is unique. Try `tz_localize("Europe/London")` and it raises. | `to_datetime(utc=True)` then `tz_convert("Europe/London")` for local features. | "How would you prove which timezone the feed is in without documentation?" |
| 2 | Daily energy — `resample("D").sum()` with missing hours | bug | `sum()` of an empty bin is 0, and days missing an hour are ~4% low. The "lowest demand day" (0 MWh on 2022-03-28) is an artefact; the whole missing day 2022-03-27 is never noticed. | `daily.nsmallest(5)` shows a 0. `df.index.to_series().diff().value_counts()` shows 52 two-hour gaps and one 25-hour gap. | Reindex to a full hourly grid, count hours per day, `sum(min_count=...)`, exclude incomplete days from rankings (fixed: 55 incomplete days). | "Why not just interpolate the missing hours?" (fine for a feature, not for a reported total without a completeness flag) |
| 3 | Daily energy — duplicates never removed | bug | 15 hours are counted twice in daily totals, but *are* removed later for the peak-hour section, so totals and shares use different data. | `df.index.duplicated().sum()` = 15; `df.duplicated().sum()` = 15. Also `hourly` is built with `~df.index.duplicated()` — ask why only there. | `drop_duplicates()` once, up front, and check whether duplicates disagree before choosing `keep=`. | "Are the duplicates identical? What if they weren't?" |
| 4 | Price — `replace("missing", np.nan)` then, after the column vanished, `pd.to_numeric(df["price_eur_mwh"].fillna(0))` | bug | `replace` leaves `object` dtype so `mean(numeric_only=True)` silently drops the column. The patch fills the 100 missing markers with 0 before converting — 0 is a legal price here (there are negative prices), so nothing flags it later. Biases the annual mean down by ~0.5 EUR/MWh. | `df.info()` shows `price_eur_mwh object`; `daily_mean.columns` lacks price; `(df.price_eur_mwh == 0).sum()` after the patch is 100 while the clean data has none. | `pd.to_numeric(errors="coerce")`, leave NaN, let `mean()` skip them. | "What would have happened if the resample had *not* dropped the column?" (TypeError; the author would have found the dtype problem earlier) |
| 5 | "average price paid" — simple `mean()` of hourly prices | choice | Price paid is load-weighted; high-load hours are high-price hours so the simple mean understates it (2022: 112.3 simple vs 114.5 weighted). | Ask what "paid" means; compute both. | `sum(price*load)/sum(load)` per day, excluding hours where either is NaN. | "Which hours dominate the difference, and does that matter for hedging?" |
| 6 | Peak hour — `idxmax().hour` on the UTC index, reported as local | bug | Peak hour reads 18 in every month; the local peak is 19 in BST months. | Peak hour is identical for all 12 months — implausible if clocks changed. | Compute on `tz_convert("Europe/London")` index (fixed: Jan 18h, Jul 19h). | "How would DST show up in a load profile?" |
| 7 | `resample("D", label="right")` "label each day with the date it completes" | bug | Every daily total is stamped with the *next* date: the 0-MWh day appears as 2022-03-28, 2022 has 364 labels and 2023 has 365, which triggers the crash in #10. | `daily.head()` starts on 2022-01-02 while the data starts 2022-01-01; `len(d2022), len(d2023)` = (364, 365). | Default `label="left"` (bin `[D 00:00, D+1 00:00)` labelled D). | "What's the difference between `label` and `closed`? When would `label='right'` be correct?" (e.g. a bar stamped with its end time for point-in-time safety) |
| 8 | Coldest day — `min()` on `temp_c` containing `-999` | suspicious | The coldest day is "-999 C"; daily `mean_temp` reaches -80. Obvious once printed, yet the author reported it. | `daily.describe()` shows `min_temp` mean -77.9; `df.temp_c.describe()` min -999. | Replace sentinels with NaN before any aggregation (63 sentinels; real coldest day 2023-01-24 at -6.4 C). | "How do you find sentinels you don't know about?" (value_counts of extreme values, histogram tails, round numbers) |
| 9 | `hourly = ... .reindex(full).ffill()` on the whole frame | choice / bug | Forward-fills 78 missing hours including the *measured quantity*: the missing day becomes 24 copies of 23:00 on 26 March, giving a flat day with peak hour 0 and evening share exactly 4/24 = 0.167 (the minimum in the output). It also drags a -999 sentinel forward. | `daily.evening_share.min()` = 0.167 = 4/24; `hourly.loc["2022-03-27"].nunique()` = 1. | Keep NaN; make aggregations NaN-aware; exclude incomplete days. | "When is `ffill` acceptable?" (slowly-varying exogenous state known to persist, never a target or a reported total) |
| 10 | YoY — `(d2023.values - d2022.values) / d2022.values` crashes; patched with `[:n]` | bug | Positional alignment: after `[:364]` day *i* of 2023 is compared with day *i* of 2022 but the label offset (#7) and the missing day mean they are one calendar day and two weekdays apart. A divide-by-zero from the 0-MWh day is thrown away with `isfinite`. The result (-0.52%) happens to be close to the honest -0.57% — right answer for the wrong reason. | The crash itself; the RuntimeWarning; `d2022.index[0]` vs `d2023.index[0]` are different month-days. | Align on month-day labels, keep only days complete in both years (312 pairs), report aggregate and mean-of-daily separately. | "Your number was almost right — so does it matter?" (yes: the method is not robust; with a different gap pattern it would be badly wrong) |
| 11 | Evening share — `between_time("17:00", "20:00")` on UTC, described as 17–20 local | bug | In BST the window is actually 18–21 local. The aggregate share barely moves here (19.33%) because the load profile is flat across 18–21, but the definition is wrong and the denominator differs from the `energy_mwh` column (deduped vs not). | Compare `daily.energy_mwh` with `hourly.consumption_mwh.resample("D").sum()`: they differ by the 15 duplicated hours. | Local index, one denominator, complete days only. | "Your number didn't change after the fix — how do you decide whether a bug matters?" |

Also worth saying aloud: the constant `region` column is useless (`nunique() == 1`), the notebook never checks the sampling interval or the row count against the expected 17,520, and `describe()` was printed but not read.
