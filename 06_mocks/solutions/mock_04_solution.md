# Mock 04 — Price signal backtest — solution

**Story:** predict the next hour's day-ahead price return from momentum, volatility and load change; trade the sign. Headline: Sharpe 12.9, hit rate 63.6%, "robust on the 6h horizon".

**Two questions unlock most of it:** *what is one unit of the target, and is it in the future of every feature?* and *when is the signal known relative to the P&L it is credited with?*

| # | Where | Kind | Why it matters | How to detect | Fix | Likely follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Load* — `df["price_eur_mwh"].pct_change()` | bug | Prices are ≤ 0 in 43 hours and within 5 EUR of zero in 27. Percentage returns through zero are meaningless: `ret.min() = -272.7`, `ret.max() = 37.6`. A handful of rows dominate every mean, std and regression. | `describe()` is printed in the notebook — read it. `(price <= 0).sum()`. | Work in EUR/MWh differences (`diff()`), or log returns on a strictly positive series if there is one. | "Why does this matter more for `vol24` than for the model coefficients?" |
| 2 | *Features and target* — `df["target"] = df["ret"].shift(1)  # next hour's return` | bug (the big one) | `shift(1)` moves data *forward*: row t receives `ret[t-1]`. The "next hour's return" is last hour's return. Every downstream metric is about predicting the past. | `df[["ret","target"]].head()` — target equals the previous row's `ret`. Sign convention: future = `shift(-h)`. | `df["chg"].shift(-1)`. | "Draw the alignment for `shift(1)` and `shift(-1)` on a 3-row example." |
| 3 | *Features and target* — `mom6 = ret.rolling(6).mean()`, `mom24 = ...` | bug (given #2) | Windows ending at t include `ret[t-1]`, which is the target. `mom6` carries it with weight 1/6 — hence the coefficient of 1.22 and R² 0.20 in-sample. With a genuinely future target the same rolling window is legitimate. | Coefficient on `mom6` far larger than anything else; in-sample R² of 0.20 on hourly returns is not credible. | Correct the target; then the window is fine. Rule: features may include t, target must start at t+1. | "Is `rolling(24).mean()` without `shift(1)` always a leak?" (No — depends on the target's timing.) |
| 4 | *Scaling and split* — `scaler.fit_transform(df[features])` before splitting | research choice | Test-set mean/std leak into the scaling. Harmless for OLS predictions here, but it is the habit that bites with regularised or distance-based models and with CV. | Order of cells: scaler fitted on `df`, split after. | `Pipeline` or fit scaler on train only. | "Does it change the OLS predictions? Why (not)?" |
| 5 | *Scaling and split* — `train_test_split(..., random_state=42)`; *robustness* — `cross_val_score(cv=5)` on `target_6h` | research choice | Shuffled split of autocorrelated hourly data. The 6h-ahead targets of adjacent rows share 5 of 6 hours, so any fold contains near-copies of the held-out labels. `cv=5` without shuffling is at least contiguous, but still has no gap at the fold edges. | Overlapping labels: `target_6h.autocorr(1)` ≈ 0.8. Test hours are interleaved with train hours. | Chronological split; `TimeSeriesSplit(gap=6)` for the 6h target. | "How big should the gap be for an h-step target?" |
| 6 | *Backtest* — `pnl = signal * y_test` | bug (look-ahead) | Because of #2, `y_test` is `ret[t-1]`: the signal formed with data up to t is credited with a return that finished at t. Even with the right target the rule must be *signal at t × change t→t+1*. | Ask: "at what time is `signal` known, and over which interval is the return earned?" | `signal.shift(1) * chg` or `signal * chg.shift(-1)`. | "What if execution takes an hour?" |
| 7 | *Backtest* — `np.sqrt(24 * 252)` | bug (units) | Hourly power data covers every day: 8,760 hours/year, not 6,048. Understates by 17% here; the general point is that the annualisation factor must match the bar frequency and calendar. | Count the rows per year. | `np.sqrt(8760)`. | "Would you annualise an hourly Sharpe at all? What does it mean when positions last one hour?" |
| 8 | *Backtest* — no transaction cost | research choice | The position changes in ~43% of hours. Costs, bid–ask, and imbalance charges are zero in the notebook. | `np.diff(signal) != 0` frequency. | Charge a cost per unit traded; show gross and net. | "What is a realistic cost for this market?" |
| 9 | *Backtest* — `hit_rate = (np.sign(pred_train) == np.sign(y_train)).mean()` | bug | Computed on the **training** set, includes rows where `y == 0` (never a hit), and is compared to an out-of-sample Sharpe in the same table as if both were OOS. | The variable is `pred_train`. | Test set, exclude zeros, report alongside a 50% baseline. | "What hit rate would a coin give with this many zeros?" |
| 10 | *Results* — never checked | omission / suspicious result | Where does the P&L come from? The 2022 gas spike and price spikes produce extreme `ret` values; a few hours can carry the result. (In the fixed version the top 1% of hours contribute 18% of gross P&L; in the broken pct_change version far more.) | Sort `pnl`, share of top 1%; P&L by month. | Concentration and per-period tables before believing an aggregate. | "Would you trade this if 2022 were excluded?" |
| 11 | *Backtest* — `pnl.cumsum().plot()` on `idx_test` from a shuffled split | bug | The Series is in shuffled order; `cumsum` accumulates in that order, and matplotlib joins points in index order, so the curve is a random walk drawn as a scribble. | The x-axis makes no sense; `pnl.index.is_monotonic_increasing` is False. | `pnl.sort_index()` before cumsum — and a chronological split in the first place. | "What does a cumulative P&L curve over a random subset of hours mean anyway?" |
| 12 | *Model* — `R2 test: -2.41` explained away as "R² is not meaningful for returns" | suspicious result | A test R² of −2.4 with a train R² of 0.20 says the model is badly wrong out of sample (driven by #1's outliers), yet the author redirects to hit rate. Refusing to explain a negative OOS R² is exactly what the interviewer is watching for. | It is printed. | Investigate: which rows drive the squared error? (the pct_change blow-ups). | "What does a negative R² mean, mechanically?" |
| 13 | whole notebook — premise | research choice | Day-ahead prices for all 24 hours of D+1 are set in a single auction on day D. There is no market in which you buy at hour t and sell at hour t+1 of the DA curve. The tradable version is a once-a-day decision or an intraday/imbalance series. | Domain knowledge. | Re-pose the problem around the decision the desk can actually make. | "How would you set up the same idea for the intraday market?" |

| 6h target CV R² | 0.255 (KFold) | 0.322 (`TimeSeriesSplit`, gap 6) |
| Sharpe | 12.9 with √(24·252), on a past return | 32.0 gross / 28.6 net of 1 EUR/MWh, √8760 |

| P&L concentration | not checked | top 1% of hours = 18% of gross P&L |

**Important for scoring yourself:** fixing the mechanics *raises* the Sharpe here. That is a property of the synthetic data — price = smooth level + independent hourly noise, so hourly changes are strongly mean-reverting (lag-1 autocorrelation −0.29) and the "momentum" model is really a noise-reversal model. The fixed notebook's last section is the part that matters: the bug-free backtest is still not a strategy, because (a) the edge is reversal of iid noise, (b) day-ahead prices cannot be traded hour by hour, (c) costs and imbalance are not modelled. A strong candidate finds the bugs **and** says "I still don't believe it, and here is why."
