# Mock 03 — Weather forecasts and point-in-time correctness — solution

**Story:** at 12:00 on day D we submit an hourly consumption forecast for D+1. The author compares actual temperature, "the latest forecast" and the noon forecast and concludes the latest forecast loses only 0.05% R².

**The one question that unlocks most of this notebook:** *for a row whose target is hour T on D+1, what was actually known at 12:00 on day D?* Applied to every column it finds problems 1, 2, 7, 11 by itself.

| # | Where | Kind | Why it matters | How to detect | Fix | Likely follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Calendar features and target* — `df["temp_target"] = df["temp_c"].shift(-24)` | research choice (PIT) | Actual temperature at the target hour is only known after the fact. As a *benchmark* it is fine; as a candidate production feature it is a leak. The notebook treats it as the reference model. | Ask what `temp_target` would be at 12:00 D for T = 18:00 D+1. | Keep as a hindsight benchmark only; label it so. | "Where else in the frame does the same problem occur?" (→ 11) |
| 2 | *Forecast temperature* — `fc.sort_values("origin_datetime").groupby("forecast_datetime").last()` | bug (PIT) | "Most recent forecast for each hour" is the forecast issued closest to T — horizons 1–12 h, mostly issued at 00:00 D+1 or 12:00 D+1, i.e. *after* the 12:00 D decision. The honest set (origin ≤ 12:00 D) has horizons 12–35 h. | Merge `horizon_h` alongside and print `describe()`: min 1, max 12. Or compare `origin_datetime` with the decision time. | Filter `origin_datetime <= T.floor("D") - 12h` **before** taking `.last()`; assert `origin <= decision` after the merge. | "How would you structure the frame so this is impossible to get wrong?" (index by target hour, carry `decision_time`.) |
| 3 | *Load* — `parse_dates=["forecast_datetime"]` only; later `fc["origin_datetime"].str.contains("12:00")` | bug-prone / research choice | `origin_datetime` is the column that determines availability and it is never parsed or compared to anything. The noon filter by string also matches any noon issue, including the one 36+ h before T. | `fc.dtypes` / `fc.info()` shows `object`. | `parse_dates=["origin_datetime", "forecast_datetime"]`; filter on timestamps. | "Does the string filter do what the author thinks?" (Yes for 12:00, but it says nothing about availability.) |
| 4 | *Forecast temperature* — `time_local.dt.tz_localize(None) + 24h` joined to `forecast_datetime.dt.tz_localize(None)` (UTC wall time) | bug (subtle) | Left key is London wall-clock, right key is UTC wall-clock. In BST every join is shifted by one hour: the forecast attached to 15:00 UTC is the forecast for 14:00 UTC. Also breaks around the DST switch. | `(df.time_local.dt.tz_localize(None) - df.time.dt.tz_localize(None)).value_counts()` → 20,832 rows with +1 h. Or compare `temp_fc_latest` and `temp_target` in July: the error sd is larger than in January. | Join on tz-aware UTC timestamps; derive `hour` from the aware London time without stripping tz. | "Why store UTC and derive local features rather than the other way round?" |
| 5 | never done | omission | Forecast is +0.3 °C warm-biased. A linear model absorbs a constant bias in the intercept, but the candidate should check before assuming. | `(temp_fc - temp_actual).mean()` → +0.31. | Report bias overall and by horizon; consider bias-correcting or including horizon. | "Would a constant bias hurt a linear model? A non-constant one?" |
| 6 | *Results* — "0.05% R² lost" | suspicious result | Forecast error sd grows from 1.1 °C at 12 h to 2.4 °C at 35 h. The author's "latest" set has sd 0.82 °C because it is 1–12 h and issued after the decision. The tiny loss is an artefact of #2. | Group forecast error by `horizon_h`. | Use the honest forecast set; the honest hindsight-vs-forecast gap is ~10× larger. | "What horizon would you actually be using at 12:00 for hour 23 of D+1?" (35 h.) |
| 7 | *Train / test split* — `rng.random(len(df)) < 0.8` | research choice | Random split on hourly autocorrelated data; neighbours of every test hour are in train. Combined with #8 the *same target hour* is in both sets. | Print train/test time ranges — they overlap completely. | Chronological split (or grouped by day, or `TimeSeriesSplit`). | "How much does it matter here, given features are lags and dummies?" (Measure it.) |
| 8 | *Forecast temperature* — `df.merge(noon, ..., how="left")` | bug | Every target hour has **two** noon-issued forecasts (from 12:00 D−1 with horizon h+36, and 12:00 D with h+12). Rows double: 17,520 → 35,003. Metrics are computed on duplicated hours, and the random mask puts copies of the same hour in train and test. | `print(df.shape)` before/after — it is printed and ignored. `df["time"].duplicated().sum()`. | `validate="one_to_one"`, or pick one origin per target hour before merging; assert row count unchanged. | "Which of the two noon forecasts should you keep, and why?" (12:00 D, the fresher one that is still available.) |
| 9 | *Models* — `pred = model.predict(X[is_train])`, `r2_score(y[is_train], pred)` | research choice | All three reported R²/MAE are **in-sample**. `is_train` is defined and the test half is never used. | Read the loop; note `~is_train` appears nowhere after the split cell. | Evaluate on test only (and chronological). | "Why does it barely matter for OLS with 30 features and 14k rows — and when would it matter a lot?" |
| 10 | *cleaning* — `df["temp_fc_noon"].bfill()` | bug (minor, PIT) | Missing noon forecasts (first day) are filled with the *next* row's forecast, i.e. a value for a different hour and from the future. | `isna().sum()` before the fill; ask what value replaced it. | Leave NaN and drop, or use the older available origin. | "When is `ffill` acceptable and `bfill` not?" |
| 11 | *Calendar features and target* — `df["cons_lag"] = df["consumption_mwh"]` ("same hour previous day relative to target") | research choice (PIT, subtle) | Row t is on day D, target T = t+24 h on D+1. Consumption at t is known at 12:00 D **only for t ≤ 12:00**. For afternoon and evening hours the persistence feature is 1–11 h in the future of the decision. | Same question as #1 applied to the lag: "at 12:00 D, do I know consumption at 18:00 D?" | Use consumption at T−48 h (and T−168 h). | "How would you set this up if the decision time were 09:00 instead?" |
| 12 | *Results* — headline | suspicious result | Three numbers differing in the third decimal, all in-sample, on a duplicated frame, with a persistence feature that is itself unavailable — the comparison is between three flavours of the same leak. The recommendation ("use the latest forecast") recommends a data feed that does not exist at submission time. | Check the shape change, the horizon range, and where `~is_train` went. | Rebuild around the target hour and the decision time. | "What would you tell the desk about the *cost* of using forecasts?" |

## Honest result (from `mock_03_fixed.ipynb`, chronological test Aug–Dec 2023)

| model | R² test | MAE test (MWh) |
|---|---|---|
| actual temperature (hindsight, not available) | 0.9346 | 800 |
| **honest forecast, origin ≤ 12:00 D (deployable)** | **0.9269** | **842** |
| "latest" forecast, 1–12 h horizons (leak) | 0.9325 | 814 |
| no temperature | 0.9021 | 974 |

Broken notebook: in-sample R² 0.9192 / 0.9187 / 0.9157, "0.05% lost". Honest gap hindsight → deployable forecast is 0.0077 R² and 42 MWh MAE — about 15× the loss the notebook claims — and the recommended "latest forecast" is not an option at 12:00. The forecast is biased +0.31 °C; its error sd is 1.11 °C at 12 h and 2.4 °C at 35 h versus 0.82 °C for the leaked 1–12 h set.

The absolute R² level is not the point (the fixed model is stronger because it adds a week lag and its test period is easier). The point is that the comparison in the broken notebook is between leaks.
