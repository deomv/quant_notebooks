# Mock 14 — Settlement periods & DST: solution

Notebook: `mock_14_settlement_periods_and_dst.ipynb`. Fixed version: `mock_14_fixed.ipynb`.
12 planted problems. Kinds: **bug** = wrong output, **choice** = runs but methodologically wrong,
**suspicious** = a result you should refuse to accept.

| # | Where | Kind | Why it matters | How to detect | Fix | Likely follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Convert to UTC timestamps" — `pd.to_datetime(settlement_date) + (period-1)*30min` | bug | `settlement_date` is a *local* (Europe/London) date. Adding the period offset to a naive midnight gives local wall-clock time, then everything downstream calls it UTC: every hour Apr–Oct is shifted by one. | `hh.groupby("settlement_date").settlement_period.max().value_counts()` shows 46 and 50 — only a local-date file has those. `hh["utc"].dt.tz` is None. | `pd.to_datetime(date).dt.tz_localize("Europe/London").dt.tz_convert("UTC") + offset`. | "How do you know the file is local and not UTC?" (period counts on clock-change days) |
| 2 | Same cell, DST days | bug | On 2023-10-29 periods 47–50 spill onto 2023-10-30 00:00–01:30 and collide with that day's periods 1–4; on 2023-03-26 the day ends at 22:30. Nobody looks. | `hh.duplicated(["meter_id","utc"]).sum()` > 40 after building `utc` (collisions on top of the 40 real duplicates); `describe()` printed `max 50` and was not read. | Same as #1: localising handles both days; assert `utc` unique per meter. | "What does period 49 mean?" |
| 3 | No `drop_duplicates()` anywhere | bug | 40 duplicated rows are summed into hourly, annual and peak figures. | `hh.duplicated().sum()` = 40. | Dedupe first; check whether duplicates disagree before choosing `keep=`. | "Identical duplicates — does it still matter?" (yes for sums) |
| 4 | "Hourly load per meter" — `resample("h").mean()` labelled `kwh_per_hour` | bug | kWh is energy; two half-hours in an hour *add*. The mean halves every hourly value, so every downstream hourly figure (peaks, evening share) is half what it says. | Compare `hourly.kwh_per_hour.sum()` with `hh.kwh.sum()`: factor 2. | `resample("h").sum(min_count=2)` and keep a count of half-hours per hour. | "When would `mean` be right?" (for kW, i.e. average power) |
| 5 | "Largest customers" — M100007 at 285,046 kWh | suspicious | A residential meter reporting 70× its contracted 3,982 kWh. Its September median is 744× its other months: the file is in Wh for that month. It then dominates the portfolio peak (602 of 641 kW) and the September evening share (0.276 vs 0.19 elsewhere). | `hh.groupby(["meter_id", month]).kwh.median().unstack()`; the monthly evening-share table jumps in September. | Detect and divide by 1000, or exclude the meter-month and flag it. | "How would you detect a unit change you didn't know about?" |
| 6 | Stuck week for M100003 counted as valid | bug | 336 identical readings of 0.187 kWh in June are treated as consumption. Small in kWh here; in a settlement context a stuck meter is an estimation/billing issue. | `groupby(["meter_id","settlement_date"]).kwh.nunique()` shows 7 days with one distinct value. | Drop or flag stuck days; annualise the meter's total. | "What other patterns indicate a faulty meter?" |
| 7 | "M100011 ... efficiency gain" | suspicious | The meter is missing 2023-04-10 to 04-23 (coverage 96.1%). Its ratio of 1.008 vs 1.044 for its peers is the gap, not behaviour. | `hh.groupby("meter_id").size()/17520`; a per-meter calendar heatmap; the hourly summary was truncated with `head(8)` before M100011 appears, so the gap never shows. | Annualise by coverage (fixed: 1.048, in line with peers) or exclude. | "How would you tell efficiency from missing data?" |
| 8 | "Evening share" — `hour.between(17, 20)` on the mislabelled hours, benchmark "22% for the residential book" | bug / choice | `between` is inclusive: 17, 18, 19, **20** = four hours, not the three-hour 17–20 window. The hours are local-as-UTC, so in summer the window is really 18–21. The share is computed over *all* meters (SMEs are daytime-heavy) but compared to a residential benchmark. | Definition check: `hourly[hourly.hour.between(17,20)].hour.unique()` has 4 values. | Local hours, `isin([17,18,19])`, residential subset (fixed: 15.9% all, 23.0% residential). | "Your number moved from 24.9% to 23.0% — which effects pushed which way?" |
| 9 | Reconciliation — `where(m.index % 7 != 3, str.lower())` then `merge(how="inner")` | bug | Three ids are lower-case on the CRM side, upper-cased only on the settlement side; the inner join silently drops them (17 reconciled, the author never asks why not 20). | `len(recon)` = 17; `merge(..., indicator=True)` shows `left_only`. | Normalise both keys, `how="left"`, `validate="one_to_one"`, assert the count. | "Why is `inner` the dangerous default here?" |
| 10 | "Peak kW per meter is the maximum hourly load" — max of `kwh_per_hour` | bug | A half-hourly kWh reading is average power × 0.5 h, so kW = 2 × half-hourly kWh; hourly kWh (if summed) already equals average kW. Because of #4 the value is the mean of two half-hours, so the reported peak is half the true hourly average power and at most a quarter of the true half-hourly peak in kW. | Units in the column names: the author never writes down what a settlement value *is*. | `kw = kwh * 2` on the half-hourly data (fixed: individual peaks sum to 103.5 kW). | "What changes if settlement moves to 15 minutes?" |
| 11 | "capacity requirement is the sum of the individual peaks" | choice | Meters do not peak simultaneously. The coincident portfolio peak is 55.9 kW vs 103.5 kW sum of peaks (diversity factor 0.54); the broken notebook's 641 kW is 11× the honest number because of #5 too. | Compute `hh.groupby("utc").kw.sum().max()`. | Coincident peak on instants where all meters report. | "How would you size for a *future* portfolio you cannot observe?" |
| 12 | Reconciliation table — M100013 at 0.51 goes unremarked | suspicious | M100013 has solar: the settlement file is *net* import (negative at midday). Comparing net import with a gross annual estimate is not a reconciliation. | `hh.groupby("meter_id").kwh.min()` shows −0.81 for one meter; `meters.has_solar`. | Report import and export separately, or compare against a net estimate. | "What else in the file would change for an exporter?" |

## Scoring

12 problems. 8+ with correct reasoning = strong. #1/#2 (local vs UTC), #4 (mean vs sum) and #5 (the Wh month) are the core: missing any of them means the report is wrong by a factor. #10/#11 are the energy-domain checks (units and coincidence) an interviewer at a retailer will probe. Credit for #12 only if you asked what a negative kWh means.
